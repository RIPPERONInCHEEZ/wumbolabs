WumboLabs is my corner of the internet for local AI, Linux, homelabs, self-hosting, and building useful things.

What started as curiosity around running AI models locally turned into a much larger technical practice: building servers, maintaining Docker stacks, migrating storage, testing language models, documenting systems, and troubleshooting problems that rarely fail in convenient ways.

My current focus is practical local AI.

I am less interested in benchmark leaderboards and marketing claims, and more interested in answering a simpler question:

**What can normal people actually run on consumer hardware?**

That question led me to build Monolith, a local AI workbench designed to make model testing, evaluation, comparison, and experimentation easier.

## Background

My technical work started professionally in support and network operations. I worked in technical support and NOC-style roles troubleshooting wireless networks, hardware, configuration issues, outages, vendor coordination, field technician support, ticket documentation, and customer-facing communication.

That experience shaped how I approach systems:

- isolate the failure point
- document what changed
- avoid guessing when evidence is missing
- communicate clearly
- keep rollback paths available

Before that, I worked in kitchens and moved into leadership as a Sous Chef. That background still influences how I work: stay organized, stay calm under pressure, and solve problems quickly when things break.

## How I Work

I prefer stable, documented, reproducible systems over hype-driven experimentation.

My general style is:

- stability before novelty
- incremental changes over risky rewrites
- documentation before memory
- evidence before assumptions
- clear rollback paths
- practical tools over fragile automation

Most of the things I build start with a simple thought:

**I wonder if this will work.**

Sometimes it does.

Sometimes it fails spectacularly.

Either way, there is usually something worth learning.
